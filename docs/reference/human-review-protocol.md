### Human checkpoints — when agents must involve the human

**Agents must explicitly tag/notify the human when a checkpoint below is reached.** Prepare the concrete evidence or grading packet first; a document saying “human review pending” is not itself a request. Human preferences, semantic standards, judgments and consequential launch decisions must not be silently supplied by an agent. Prior authorization and answered decisions persist within their recorded scope; do not ask again merely because a new harness starts.

| Gate | When the human is needed | What the agent brings / asks | What waits, and what may continue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 — Research standards | Before fresh benchmark scoring or declaring results acceptable; when a material scope/quality tradeoff changes | Proposed tasks/coverage, example correct and incorrect outcomes, acceptable misses/false assignments/unknown coverage, citation expectations, time/API/human-review tradeoffs and a recommendation. Ask for explicit agreement or corrections | Acceptance-based scoring/claims wait for agreed criteria. Offline fixtures, sample preparation and authorized diagnostic POCs may continue, clearly provisional |
| H2 — Human grading/calibration | First usable small sample for a new tag/role/date/filter/answer family; before treating labels as human gold; when a consequential disagreement is unresolved | A small readable source packet and rubric as below. Ask the human to label or grade concrete examples, including negatives and ambiguity; preserve their actual judgments | Human-quality calibration, gold labels and safe automatic-accept claims wait. Agents can inspect evidence, fix mechanical failures or prepare independent tasks; AI review stays provisional |

If a model returns a plausible but unsupported name/date/bill assignment, reject/hold it rather than ask the human to bless a guess. Escalate a representative cluster or policy ambiguity when it affects the research standard; do not make the human adjudicate every production row. Reuse approved guidance, sample periodically, and measure remaining review workload. If the human is unsure, retain uncertainty and seek additional evidence; a forced yes/no is not a historical fact.

#### Show inputs, expected output and actual output

Direct human instruction, 2026-10-29: “I want to see the inputs to the process, the expected output, and the actual output.” Use this order for each process/example in a diagnostic review or progress walkthrough:

1. **Inputs:** show the actual source material and the relevant instruction/settings supplied to that step. Distinguish original material, prior cleanup and context. Keep exact source/scan identities available without burying the example in metadata.
2. **Expected output:** show concretely what a satisfactory result would contain, including the proposed reading or required fields/edits when available. Identify its basis: agreed requirement, human-reviewed reference, source-inspected agent illustration or synthetic test expectation. An illustration is not independent truth. When the correct wording is unknown, state that and describe the required uncertainty behavior instead of inventing an answer.
3. **Actual output:** show the saved result unchanged, with status and measured limits. Say explicitly if the process has not run or no result exists. Never replace a failed reply with a repaired demonstration.
4. **Differences:** put expected and actual differences next to the example in plain English. Include missing, incorrect and unsupported changes as well as matching parts; separate format/replay checks from historical accuracy. Give each section a comment box through evidence-local notes.

**Separate invented tests from actual observations.** In human-facing examples, label invented uncertainty or failure scenarios in the heading as invented software tests. Use clearly fictional cases without real sample identifiers; do not reuse labels such as S5/P1 in an invented display. Show actual model output separately and state explicitly when an example’s uncertainty phrase was written by the agent rather than observed in a scan or returned by a model. Internal source-bound regression fixtures may retain their required identities; this rule governs how they are presented to the human.

For an explicitly blind grading stage, preserve the source-only packet and capture expectations before exposing any model output; present the comparison in the subsequent diagnostic stage. Preserve immutable packets/answers when changing presentation by registering a new version. Continue using generous neutral glossary help in AI explanations while all historical/OCR/proposed reading excerpts remain plain. This presentation preference grants no model-call, quality-policy or full-run authority.

#### Small work samples while work is underway

Direct human instruction, 2026-10-30: “But you should put small samples of your work in the human review desk so I have the opportunity to check your work in flight and steer the direction of the project if I see details I don’t like”.

Publish a small set of real work examples (normally one to three) as soon as the first usable result exists, before expanding to more cases or substantial implementation. Do not wait for a milestone, completed batch or polished success report. Show the actual inputs, concrete expected behavior and its basis, unchanged actual output, differences/known limits and evidence-local comment boxes; include a failure or uncertainty when observed. Label partial/preliminary results plainly. A plan without an actual output is not a completed work sample; if no result exists yet, say so.

For every bounded per-model/per-case consistency experiment, plan at least three independent identical-input observations (human reaffirmation2026-11-02); predeclare any additional repeats and their bounded purpose. Show the first completed observation as preliminary and add the remaining actual results through a new immutable packet version. Do not present a single observation as the completed consistency test. Keep wrong, invalid, uncertain and known-truncated replies visible; label a safety-stopped1/3group incomplete rather than implying consistent behavior. Keep the exact request fixed across the planned repetitions. If human feedback changes the required input or method, stop or version the affected work explicitly; preserve the incomplete original group and never combine changed-input results as three identical runs.

Notify the human in chat with the verified LAN desk link when a sample is available. Check submitted feedback at that checkpoint and before the next dependent action, including each remaining model repetition or expansion to more cases; read and record every relevant evidence-local note under the existing application protocol. Incorporate actionable steering before the affected work. A versioned follow-up should explain concretely what changed in response. Existing HR014/HR015 examples remain available; do not create a new review just to ask the human to agree with this standing instruction.

These are optional opportunities to steer autonomous work. Do not impose an answer, grade or permission requirement for each small test, infer approval from silence, or wait indefinitely for comments. Continue bounded authorized work while checking for submitted feedback; huge runs and genuine unresolved decisions retain their explicit gates. No background monitor, recurring workload or automatic model call is created by this cadence.

#### How to ask for sample grades

1. Freeze IDs, raw text/context, citations, vocabulary/rubric and sample version. Start calibration with a few readable examples (typically 3–5 when suitable): a clear positive, a negative/incidental mention, an ambiguous/truncated/mixed case, and a model disagreement or failure. This calibrates expectations; it is not sufficient evidence of corpus accuracy. Fresh benchmark selection still needs the declared coverage/exclusions.
2. For **blind expectations**, let the human label the source before seeing model outputs, scores or which arm produced them. Ask what is relevant, who speaks versus is mentioned, what date/subject/bill is evidenced, or whether the question is answerable. Permit unknown, OOV, mixed and “need more context.” For **diagnostic output grading**, then show source → proposal → exact cited evidence and ask acceptable/incorrect/unclear, with the reason and severity. Do not call output-informed grades blind gold.
3. Ask a focused research question rather than requiring JSON editing: for example, “Is procedure a substantive subject here or just salary-debate context?” Provide source links/page references and enough surrounding text. Page-image access is available for the human, but this does not create a systematic visual re-OCR requirement. Bundle related decisions and estimate the review effort.
4. Preserve the human's actual answers, reviewer/time, packet/configuration versions and evidence IDs; link them in the register. A host may translate those answers to the existing validated import format, preserving the original response. Never fabricate a human identity, fill missing judgments with AI agreement, or convert an agent's source expectations into human labels.
5. Record disagreements and any adjudication. If only one human grader is available, state that limitation; a second AI reviewer is not a second human. Rule changes after seeing results require versioned criteria and fresh evaluation before claiming generalization.
6. Apply approved guidance to the next bounded sample and show whether it resolves the human's concerns. Ask again only for a genuinely new unresolved decision, changed scope/evidence, or the next scheduled calibration gate—not merely for another model call.

#### Human-facing review desk

**New reviews use the Streamlit browser desk (`make review`), not editable Markdown.** `HUMAN_REVIEW.md` is an operator/archive pointer. Use the same HR IDs/versions as the register below. SQLite holds immutable packet versions, saved drafts, human submission revisions and application receipts; this master remains the only accepted-policy/progress plan.

Every harness startup runs `uv run globe review inbox` before choosing work. The inbox returns the latest unapplied submission per reviewer; prior revisions remain exportable and cannot override newer answers. A separate human chat notification is unnecessary. Agents process submissions on their next work turn; no background agent is started by the app.

Author a concrete JSON packet under tracked `docs/review-packets/` using `uv run globe review schema`, register it, then notify the human. Include HR ID/version, question IDs, source excerpts with exact composite source IDs/text hashes/citations, review scope/effort, what waits/continues, options including uncertainty, and spending/runtime limits where relevant. Material changes require a new version. **Blind packets must contain source-only questions/evidence and omit model outputs, scores and recommendations everywhere in their payload.** The schema's `blind` flag records the author declaration; software cannot determine whether prose semantically leaks an answer. No default choice or AI-authored human answer is allowed.

**Evidence-local feedback is required work input.** The human explicitly requested that comments beside each scan be taken seriously by the model/AI process. Each submitted nonblank note appears in `review inbox` as `evidence_feedback`, with its exact comment, question, item number and unchanged evidence label/text/URL; original keyed answers and packet hashes remain authoritative. Before dependent model requests, prompt changes, repair decisions or quality claims, read every relevant submitted note and link it to its evidence. Do not silently discard comments, summarize away the cited word, or let a model's agreement override a human-reported issue. Preserve earlier failures such as an unchanged split word rather than replacing the old output.

For **every** nonblank submitted note, record a disposition in the master task evidence before marking the submission applied: the exact note ID/comment; the requested concern; action taken and a meaningful regression/check where applicable; or the precise reason/evidence it is unresolved or not adopted. Acknowledging receipt alone does not close an actionable issue. Keep unresolved concerns visible in the next bounded task and in any affected model request's source-linked instructions/checks; block a dependent acceptance claim until addressed. Human feedback is a serious input, not automatic historical truth: preserve disagreements, verify the source, and explain uncertainty rather than making an unsupported canonical edit. Drafts are saved work, not submitted judgments; the UI makes that distinction explicit. Agents consume submitted notes on their next work turn, not through an automatic background run.

For a completed submission: export the exact packet/answers to a new JSON archive, verify its packet hash and revision status, apply the human's judgment narrowly in this plan/HR register, and commit the archive plus plan. Then record `review applied` with the real commit reference and export its receipt to a separate new JSON path; commit that receipt. Do not mark applied before a reviewable committed change exists. Keep every answer/revision immutable, preserve uncertainty, and never treat diagnostic feedback as blanket name repair, P0 closure or full-run approval. Exports use canonical JSON packet bytes plus SHA-256, not the author's original file whitespace; keep the authored JSON in Git as well. Generic free-text verdicts do not satisfy `subject_review.py`'s vocabulary/window/span-label import contract.

Legacy Markdown/chat answers retain their existing exact-wording archive/receipt procedure. HR-005/008 originals and their hash receipts remain immutable/read-only; never re-present them as pending, pretend they were submitted through the web, or ask them again. Human-reported image readings remain distinct from verified corpus patches. Match the HR register, actual new answers and applied follow-up before choosing dependent work; preserve unresolved required questions without forcing answers. A submission is not an automatic model request or an automatic canonical-text edit.

#### Mandatory plain-language and glossary rule

Direct human instruction, 2026-10-26: “I need even more words underlined with glossary definitions. Do it now and make it a rule.” Apply this to every new or revised human-facing review, not just HR-010. The human clarified: “I do NOT need underlined vocab stuff for the original 1800s writing!!! I need it for your AI sentences that you write to me in the review desk!!” This clarification limits glossary help to AI-written review prose.

- Write the question and instructions in ordinary English first. A glossary is additional help, not permission to use unexplained jargon. Use a real or explicitly labelled illustrative example when an explanation would otherwise remain abstract.
- Generously define unfamiliar words, abbreviations and phrases in the AI’s own review-desk sentences. Inspect AI-written titles, instructions, question prompts, answer choices, evidence labels and explanations. Original historical writing, OCR quotations and proposed transcriptions must remain plain, without glossary buttons—even when they contain catalog terms. In a mixed evidence card, keep help in the AI explanation while leaving the quoted/transcribed portions plain. Explain phrases such as “paragraph boundaries,” “fixed selection,” “raw reply,” “provenance” and “faithful,” rather than assuming the reviewer knows them.
- **Glossary access, direct human rule2026-11-02:** never directly read or dump `docs/review-glossary.json` into agent context (including cat/sed/whole-file search output). Use Python JSON queries and create/read/update/delete operations instead; load/validate internally and return only requested terms, bounded matches or concise change summaries. Preserve unrelated entries, check term/alias collisions and neutral definitions; do not change immutable packet/answer bytes. This applies to every agent, including delegates.
- Put reusable definitions and displayed singular/plural/hyphenated variants in `docs/review-glossary.json`; use packet-level `glossary` only for a genuine context-specific meaning. Existing packet overrides remain authoritative for that packet. Source text stays verbatim and has no glossary underlining. `docs/review-source-spans.json` records explicit Unicode character ranges of historical/transcription text by the exact evidence-body SHA-256; classify every evidence body when authoring it. Use an empty range list only for a wholly AI-written explanation; list plain source/transcription spans for a quoted or mixed card. Unknown or changed evidence-body hashes render entirely plain until explicitly classified, so a missed entry can never add vocabulary links to historical writing. Do not infer authorship from a word’s vocabulary or whether a card uses source styling. The shared evidence renderer annotates only text outside these ranges; validate the actual text and displayed boundaries. For a submitted/registered immutable packet, improve the shared catalog rather than editing its bytes or making the human review it again.
- Keep definitions short, concrete and neutral. Do not declare a proposal correct, suggest a grade, expose a hidden model or resolve an unanswered historical/policy question through a definition. Avoid turning a word with several meanings into a misleading single meaning; prefer a specific phrase or explain the relevant alternatives. In blind reviews, definitions must contain no model output or result-based hints.
- Render dotted-underlined, tap/keyboard-accessible definitions using the shared renderer. Native answer selectors cannot contain definition buttons: provide the existing clickable answer-term help above them. Definition buttons must not submit forms, rerun the app or clear answers. Keep plain text and source links usable.
- Before delivery, inspect the actual rendered packet with its custom definitions, check meaningful terms in every AI-written surface, and add missing definitions/aliases. Verify zero glossary buttons in historical/OCR/proposed-transcription spans, including mixed cards, while surrounding AI prose retains its help. Keep a regression case for the reviewed terms and verify longest-phrase matching, neutral meanings, duplicate-name checks and preservation of questions/answers. Browser verification should open/close definitions with mouse/tap and keyboard and check that a draft/unsaved answer remains. Record any unavailable device/browser check honestly. Do not demand definitions for every ordinary word or mark coverage as complete solely from a dictionary size or underline count.

The expanded shared glossary affects existing packets on the next app render without registering another packet or changing an answer. Standalone glossary definitions do not create new human questions, authorize a model run or constitute historical acceptance.

#### Request/decision protocol shared by every harness

Every human request has an ID in the register below, a concrete registered browser packet (or explicitly preserved legacy/chat packet), and a linked work unit/readiness item. Before sending it, record the concrete packet, recommendation/options, impact, exact question, blocking dependent actions and independent work that can proceed. Then notify the human through the current chat/harness; do not contact other people or create a schedule without authorization.

Use states `not_requested` (still preparing), `awaiting_human` (actually sent), `answered`, `applied`, `deferred`, or `superseded`. Record the send time/channel/reference and the actual answer/evidence/version/scope/reopening condition when available. Silence, elapsed time, an unread request, a default option or AI consensus is not an answer. On handoff, pending requests stay pending; the replacement agent checks their status rather than sending duplicates. An answer in chat must be persisted into this plan and the evidence before applying it as a durable policy for future agents.

Pause only dependent decisions/actions. Continue useful independent work under existing authorization. Do not mark the whole project blocked while an offline safe action remains. A proposed rubric can support exploratory diagnostics if clearly unapproved; it cannot certify success or close a human gate.
